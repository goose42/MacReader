The city council voted on Tuesday to approve a new plan for the waterfront, ending a debate that has lasted almost three years, according to officials who attended the session.

Supporters said the plan would bring parks, housing and jobs to a district that has sat mostly empty since the shipyards closed, while critics warned that rents nearby could rise quickly.

Construction is expected to begin next spring, with the first phase, a public promenade and a small market hall, scheduled to open to visitors within two years of the groundbreaking.

The mayor called the vote a turning point, and promised regular public reports on the budget, the schedule and the effect on the surrounding neighborhoods as the work moves ahead.
